## Project Overview
The goal of this project is to provide leadership with data-driven insights and recommendations to assess how effectively the network is performing following its launch and determine how it should evolve over the coming months and years. The findings will support decisions related to network development, operational improvements, and commercial performance, while identifying underperforming areas, operational or customer anomalies, and profitable elements that can be leveraged to support sustainable growth.


## Project Background
Borne2Win is an EV charging network company providing public charging infrastructure. The company launched its network in Paris in March 2021, with the ambition of becoming a leading EV charging network in the city before expanding its footprint across France and other European markets.

As a newly launched network, Borne2Win needs to understand how effectively its charging infrastructure is capturing demand, generating revenue, and delivering a reliable charging experience. The first several months of operations therefore provide an opportunity to assess network performance, identify performance gaps, and determine where operational or commercial improvements may be required to support sustainable growth.


Reporting to the Chief Operating Officer, this analysis evaluates the network's post-launch performance and provides insights for cross-functional teams across Finance, Product, and Operations. The objective is to understand where the network is successfully capturing demand and creating value, as well as where performance constraints may be limiting revenue growth or service reliability.


The available EV charging data covers individual charging sessions recorded between March and December 2021, providing visibility into three key areas of network performance: 

**Revenue performance** — Tracking revenue trends and revenue contribution across EVSEs and geographic zones to understand where the network generates value.

**Demand & charging performance** — Analyzing charging activity and energy consumption across time, locations, EVSEs, and cable types to identify demand patterns, underperforming assets, and opportunities to improve network utilization.

**Operational reliability** — Evaluating charging session success and failure rates across the network to identify operational performance gaps and opportunities to improve the customer charging experience.

## Key Insights


### Monthly Performance Section
Borne2Win revenue growth activity increase steadily from April to July 2021, with revenue growing every month (+10%, +8,9%, +6,9% MoM). This gradual increase consistent with an early ramp-up of a newly deployed charging network in the city. Pandemic restrictions may also have influenced activity levels.

Activity declined between July and August 21, with revenue decreasing by 14.5% MoM. The decrease may be partly explained by seasonal effects (summer holidays, lower business activity) although other factors cannot be yet ruled out.

From September to December 2021, revenue growth accelerated sharply, with an 85,3% increase in September - followed by continued but more moderate growth in the following months ; +35.5% in October, +45.9% in November and +26.3% in December. This may reflect increasing network adoption, greater awareness of the service, and the easing of pandemic-related restrictions, while the progressive moderation in monthly growth suggests a transition toward a more established level of demand.

### Regional Performance Section 

The 16th district consistently ranked as the network’s top revenue contributor, accounting for 12–16% of monthly network revenue throughout the period.This consistent performance suggests strong observed demand and makes the district a potential priority for future network development.

Despite its relatively small EVSE base, the 2nd district ranked among the top districts across all three activity-intensity metrics (Revenue/Observed EVSE: €2,468, Top 1; Consumption/Observed EVSE: 2,028 kWh, Top 2; Sessions/Observed EVSE: 190, Top 2), indicating particularly high activity per observed EVSE. Closer monitoring would help assess potential capacity constraints and identify opportunities to leverage this strong performance through targeted network development.

North-East districts (both 18th, 19th and 20th) showed relatively low activity volumes despite their substantial installed EVSE base, while recording higher energy consumption per session. 20th district as Top 1 with 13,1 kWh/Session, followed by 18th and 19th districts, with 12,8kWh and 12,6kWh respectively. This pattern merits further investigation to determine whether pricing, session duration or local charging demand could explain the difference.

The 10th district ranked last not only as activity contributor in activity intensity metrics; 754€, 58 Sessions and 702kWh per Observed EVSE. But also one of the latest in revenue, kWh and sessions count metrics (counting between 1,6%-2% contribution only). Both Finance and Product team should monitor these results over time and investigate potential causes to determine whether targeted operational or commercial measures are warranted.

### Product Performance Section

**BOOST - FLEX EVSE** 

In terms of total revenue, BOOST held a strong initial lead during the launch period, generating approximately €650K and accounting for around 74% of combined revenue in the first half of the period. However, its monthly revenue gradually declined through August.

Meanwhile, FLEX progressively narrowed the gap between May and August before recording a remarkable 680% revenue increase from August (€67K) to December (€521K), overtaking BOOST from September onward and ultimately reaching 70% of combined EVSE revenue in December.

From a revenue intensity perspective, BOOST consistently outperformed FLEX and MOTO, reaching €1,320 per observed EVSE in December, compared with €318 for FLEX and €48 for MOTO. This demonstrates substantially higher revenue generation per observed unit for BOOST, potentially reflecting differences in cable mix, charging configurations and session characteristics.

MOTO also gained traction from June onward, with charging sessions increasing steadily through December, although its overall contribution remained modest compared with BOOST and FLEX with a revenue contribution of 0,6%. Its later deployment may partly explain its lower contribution to total EVSE revenue over the period.





**Cable Performance**

BOOST EVSE 

T2 accounted for 53% of total BOOST revenue, while Combo CCS and CHAdeMO combined contributed 33%. 
This underscores T2’s leading role in both usage and revenue generation, despite BOOST EVSE being equipped with dedicated high-power connectors designed for high-power charging sessions, such as Combo CCS and CHAdeMO. 

T3 remained a minor BOOST contributor, despite a sharp increase from €10.5K in October to €44.8K in November, followed by a decline to €13.4K in December. This unusual spike warrants further investigation to determine whether it reflects increased charging activity, changes in session characteristics, or a potential data issue.

EF generated comparatively modest revenue throughout the period, highlighting a potential area for further investigation into its activity levels and revenue contribution.

FLEX EVSE

T2S revenue surged from September onward, overtaking EF and T2 to become FLEX’s leading revenue contributor in October and reaching approximately €320K in December, representing 61% of total FLEX revenue. 

Deployed in June, T2S rapidly gained prominence, shifting FLEX’s revenue mix away from EF and T2 and highlighting a potential growth opportunity. This trend points to T2S’s growth potential, potentially reflecting the deployment of additional T2S-equipped EVSE and increasing consumer adoption and awareness of Borne2Win’s CPO network.

Further investigation into session volumes, consumption and EVSE deployment is needed to determine whether this growth stems from increased T2S usage, broader network activity or an expanded deployment base.
